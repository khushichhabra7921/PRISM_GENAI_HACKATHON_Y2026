This is the Smart Guided Troubleshooting Engine, our prototype for Theme 2 of the Samsung PRISM Generative AI Hackathon.

It turns a vague Galaxy complaint into a validated, ordered troubleshooting plan, where every step comes from trusted support content, and every fix is one tap away.

People describe symptoms, but Settings speak in menus.

Today, an agent reads the knowledge base, picks the steps, and orders them by hand. That takes about fifteen minutes per scenario, and the user still has to hunt through nested menus.

Our targets: under three hundred milliseconds for known issues, under eight seconds for new ones, zero invented steps, and zero invalid deeplinks.

The design principle is simple. Language models reason, retrieval grounds, and deterministic code validates.

A complaint first goes to a semantic cache. On a miss, it is enriched, grounded in support articles with hybrid retrieval, and turned into steps that are copied from the source text.

The steps are ordered from safe settings to critical actions, mapped to the exact Settings screen, and checked by eleven validators before the plan is cached.

Here is the demo interface. It runs entirely on a laptop CPU, using free, local, open models.

First complaint: swipe gestures go the wrong way after installing an app.

The engine returns one automatic action with five grounded steps.

The Open button carries the exact catalog deeplink, for Display, Navigation bar, not the parent Display menu.

It came from the cache in a few dozen milliseconds, with zero language model calls, and zero cost.

Next, a paraphrase the system has never seen: there is a weird flicker on my display.

The semantic cache still recognises it, so the validated screen flickering plan comes back instantly, again without any model call.

Now, two problems in one sentence: my screen flickers, and the battery dies fast.

The engine splits the complaint, grounds each half in its own support article, and returns two goals.

Within each goal, safe settings come first, physical checks next, and disruptive steps, such as safe mode or a restart, come last.

Every action carries an evidence trail.

It shows the source sentence each step was copied from, the retrieval and rerank scores, the runner-up screen, and the confidence tier, so a support team can see exactly why each step was chosen.

When the knowledge base has no answer, the engine refuses to guess.

My Galaxy Watch won't sync my steps returns an empty plan with a no match flag, and the query is logged, so the content team can find the gaps.

A brand new complaint goes through the full pipeline: sound only comes out of one earbud.

Retrieval, grounded extraction, ordering, deeplink mapping, and validation finish in well under a second, on the CPU.

And the response is pure JSON that follows the schema contract exactly.

We measured everything with our benchmark, on a synthetic starter kit.

100 percent of outputs are schema valid and pass all eleven rule checks, with zero URL leaks, and every deeplink comes from the catalog.

Cached answers return in 23 milliseconds at the ninety-fifth percentile, new queries in about 428 milliseconds, and 84 percent of unseen paraphrases are served from the cache, without any model call.

All 34 tests pass, including the five gold samples.

The main limitations: the results come from a synthetic kit, about 6 percent of paraphrase hits served a neighbouring plan, and the small local model is too slow on a CPU for the request path, so it writes cache paraphrases in the background instead.

Everything is open source, starts with docker compose up, and costs nothing to run. Thank you.
